Brain Data and Privacy

Safety, Privacy, and Identity

Brain data is deeply personal because it reflects a person’s thoughts, emotions, memories, and identity. As technology advances, it becomes more important to protect this information with strong safety and privacy rules. People should have control over who can access their brain data, how it is used, and whether it is stored or shared at all. This is especially important because brain data can reveal private details about a person’s mental health, behavior, and even their sense of self. If someone’s brain data is collected without clear consent, it can lead to serious risks, including exploitation, discrimination, and loss of autonomy. In the end, the person whose brain data it is should have the right to decide what happens to it, and that data should not be treated as property that others can claim or use without permission.
